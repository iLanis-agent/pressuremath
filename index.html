Pressure math

Pressure washers are sold on a number measured where it costs nothing: PSI at zero flow. The cleaning happens somewhere else - in PSI times GPM, the cleaning units the poster never mentions. And the machine's working pressure is not set by the motor at all, but by the tiny orifice in the nozzle you snap in. Pressure math does the honest arithmetic: box numbers become cleaning units, your pump becomes the right nozzle size, and your driveway becomes a time estimate that does not come from a commercial. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

The three tools

  • Cleaning units - PSI and GPM become the figure that cleans, banded from rinser to industrial, with a flag when the box is selling pressure more than cleaning.
  • Nozzle size - your flow and target pressure become the orifice number to buy, rounded to the nearest half size.
  • Time plan - cleaning units and area become honest minutes with a surface cleaner and with a wand.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: bands and coverage rates are starting points, not promises - surface, nozzle distance and technique move the real time. Never point the jet at people or pets, respect the kickback on ladders, and check what the surface can take: a zero-degree tip writes its name in soft wood, and high pressure can force water under skin, which is a medical emergency, not a scratch.
